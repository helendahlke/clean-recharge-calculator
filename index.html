What is residual soil nitrate-N?

Residual soil nitrate-N is the nitrate nitrogen remaining in the soil after crop uptake. It can be measured through soil sampling and laboratory analysis. Ask your soil-testing laboratory or crop adviser for the residual nitrate-N inventory in pounds of N per acre for the sampled depth (for example, 0–3 feet). The selections and custom input show a nitrate-N amount per acre, not a soil concentration in ppm (mg/kg).

If your lab reports nitrate-N in ppm (mg/kg dry soil), you also need the sampling depth and soil bulk density to convert that value into lb N/acre. When converting measured soil nitrate-N concentrations (mg N/kg dry soil) to residual nitrate-N inventories (lb N/acre), we assume a soil profile depth of 3 feet (0.91 m) and a dry soil bulk density of 1.6 g/cm³. These assumptions are not required when the residual nitrate-N inventory is already known in lb N/acre. Use your measured sampling depth and bulk density when available. Do not enter ppm as lb N/acre.

Calculation method and important assumptions

This calculator treats the selected nitrate-N inventory as completely exported and uniformly diluted into the entire applied water volume. For a nitrate inventory of M (lb N/acre) and water depth D (feet), estimated average concentration is C = 0.3678 × M / D (mg/L NO₃-N). The inverse calculation gives the water depth needed for an idealized average concentration of 10 mg/L.

  • Incoming recharge water is assumed to contain zero nitrate-N.
  • All applied water is assumed to become deep percolation; initial soil water deficits and evaporation are ignored.
  • The entire selected soil nitrate-N inventory is assumed to flush and mix uniformly; the initial nitrate pulse may be much more concentrated than the average.
  • Preferential flow, nitrate transformation, soil-water storage, deeper nitrate, groundwater mixing and travel time are not modeled.
  • The nitrate inventory must correspond to the soil depth being evaluated. Profile depth and bulk density are not needed in the calculation once nitrate-N is expressed in lb N/acre.
  • The 10 mg/L NO₃-N line is the U.S. drinking-water maximum contaminant level for nitrate expressed as nitrogen; it is not a guarantee of safe recharge or a regulatory compliance determination.

This is an educational screening tool, not a field-specific recharge application recommendation. In particular, increasing applied water to meet an average dilution target does not ensure the initial nitrate-rich leachate remains below 10 mg/L.
